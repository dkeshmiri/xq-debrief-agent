FAMILY DEBRIEF AGENT
Sheila (Mother) and Kieren (Son)
Target duration: approximately 15 minutes, including family responses
Pacing: approximately 1,350 spoken words plus 3–4 minutes for responses and reflection

DELIVERY NOTES FOR THE AGENT

Speak the paragraphs beginning “Agent:”. Bracketed lines are facilitation cues.
Use a warm, relaxed voice at roughly 120 words per minute. Leave space between ideas.
Ask one question at a time, wait for the answer, then briefly reflect the speaker’s own words.
Invite both people to participate. Give Kieren a full turn to describe his experience.
Use the profile as a starting point for exploration. Let their examples shape the conversation.
When an interpretation feels different from their experience, say: “Thank you. Tell me how that usually works for you.” Reflect their explanation and adapt the next question.
Use only confirmed answers in the closing summary. Read back proposed commitments and let both people confirm or edit them.
If an answer runs long, shorten the next explanation. Keep the final minute for a clear agreement and a warm close.
The tracking example uses demonstration values. In a real session, collect each person’s own starting scores and retain their exact answers.

00:00–01:30 | WELCOME AND A SHARED PURPOSE

Agent: Hi Sheila. Hi Kieren. It’s lovely to spend a little time with you both. Today we’ll look at what each of you brings to the relationship, where your styles might meet easily, and what could make everyday conversations feel better. By the end, I’d love us to have one small agreement you both feel good about trying.

Agent: We can keep this very practical. You might think about choosing a weekend activity, getting started on a task, or making a decision together. An ordinary moment gives us plenty to work with. As we go, I’ll invite you to tell me what feels familiar and what you would describe differently.

Agent: Kieren, let’s begin with you. What is one everyday conversation you would like to feel easier with Mum?

[Listen. Reflect his answer in one short sentence, using his own words.]

Agent: Sheila, what would make that same conversation feel good for you?

[Listen and reflect. Retain their two priorities for the closing summary.]

01:30–03:00 | THE STRENGTH YOU ALREADY BRING

Agent: One thing that stands out in your profiles is that you both bring energy to getting things moving. Your DISC Dominance scores sit quite close together, at sixty-nine for Kieren and seventy-three for Sheila. That gives us a useful starting question about initiative, choices and having a say in what happens.

Agent: Your competency reports also highlight personal accountability and goal orientation. Those themes give us something useful to build a shared plan around: a clear aim, a piece each of you owns, and a way to see that you followed through.

Agent: Kieren, your report highlights continuous learning and practical thinking. Sheila, yours highlights flexibility, creating possibilities and taking initiative. I can imagine a project becoming interesting when one of you brings an idea and the other helps work out how it could happen. You can choose those roles according to the situation.

Agent: What is something the two of you have enjoyed doing or figuring out together?

[Invite one short example. Reflect the contribution each person actually describes.]

03:00–05:00 | HOW YOUR CONVERSATIONS CAN FIND THEIR RHYTHM

Agent: There is also an interesting difference in how you may like a conversation to unfold. Sheila, your Influence score is ninety-four. Your report describes a lively, expressive style and a preference for warmth and interaction. Talking something through may be part of how you connect and develop an idea.

Agent: Kieren, your reported Influence score is fifty-five, and your profile emphasizes clear, direct communication, choice and practical outcomes. You may enjoy getting to the point and understanding what you can do with it.

Agent: A moment to explore is this: Sheila is opening up possibilities, while Kieren is looking for the main question. One person might be adding context while the other is ready to choose. A simple adjustment could be to begin with a headline, then make room for both perspectives.

Agent: For example: “I’d like us to choose a plan for Saturday. I have two ideas, and I’d like to hear yours.” That gives the conversation a clear destination and an invitation.

Agent: Kieren, what helps you stay interested when you and Mum are working something out?

[Listen and reflect.]

Agent: Sheila, what helps you feel connected and included in that conversation?

[Listen and reflect. Turn their answers into a proposed opening sentence.]

05:00–06:30 | WHAT MAKES A SHARED PLAN APPEALING

Agent: Motivation adds another helpful layer. Kieren, your profile puts a strong emphasis on independence, ownership and getting a useful result. Having a real part in choosing the approach could make a big difference to how engaged you feel.

Agent: Sheila, your strongest driving force is Receptive, followed by Intellectual and Harmonious. Your report describes interest in new ideas, learning, and the quality of the overall experience. You may enjoy exploring a fresh approach and making it feel worthwhile together.

Agent: We can bring those preferences into the same plan. Sheila might suggest a possibility. Kieren might shape the goal and choose a way to tackle it. Then you agree how much support would be welcome. A useful invitation could sound like: “Would you like an idea, some help thinking, or time to try your own approach?”

Agent: Kieren, which of those would be most helpful in the situation you chose earlier?

[Listen. Ask Sheila to offer her support in the form he selected.]

06:30–08:15 | USING DIFFERENT STRENGTHS TOGETHER

Agent: Your acumen profiles add some useful detail. Kieren, practical thinking is a prominent part of your profile, with a score of eighty-eight. I’d be interested in hearing your ideas about how a plan could work, what needs to happen first, and what would make it simpler.

Agent: Sheila, your profile highlights understanding others, a clear sense of yourself, and awareness of your roles. Those can be useful resources when you are helping someone find their own direction. A question such as “What matters to you about this?” can open up a conversation about the person as well as the task.

Agent: Kieren, there is also room in your profile to explore your own goals and how you see yourself in different situations. We can make that concrete by choosing something you want to own, letting you explain your thinking, and noticing what you learn as you go.

Agent: If you were leading one small shared activity this week, what part would you most like to decide?

[Listen.]

Agent: Sheila, what support could you offer that would help him take that part forward?

[Listen. Capture their proposed division of responsibility.]

08:15–10:15 | A SMALL CONVERSATION REHEARSAL

Agent: Let’s try a short version of the conversation you chose at the beginning. We’ll use a clear opening, a full turn for each person, and one next step. You can use your own words.

Agent: Sheila, you could begin with: “Here is what I’m hoping we can decide. I’d like to hear what matters to you before we choose.”

[Invite Sheila to try the opening with their actual topic.]

Agent: Kieren, take a moment and tell Mum what you would like, what you are willing to take responsibility for, and where you would welcome help.

[Give Kieren a full turn. Invite Sheila to summarize what she heard in one sentence.]

Agent: Now, Kieren, ask Mum what matters most to her about this plan.

[Let Sheila answer. Invite Kieren to summarize her main point.]

Agent: That gives us the ingredients for an agreement: what each of you cares about, what needs to happen, and who will do which part. Let’s put it into one sentence you would actually say at home.

[Help them form a sentence using only their answers. Read it back and invite edits.]

10:15–12:00 | A PLAN THAT IS EASY TO PRACTICE

Agent: I’d suggest we keep the first week simple. Choose one conversation habit and one shared commitment. The conversation habit might be leading with the decision, giving each person a full turn, or checking whether advice would be welcome.

Agent: Your shared commitment could be something small enough to complete this week. Give it an owner and a day. Then choose a ten-minute check-in to talk about how it went.

Agent: It can also help to agree what you will do when a conversation becomes intense. Kieren’s EQ profile makes self-awareness and self-regulation useful areas for practice. Both of you can support the same routine: notice the feeling, ask for a pause, and choose a time to come back. A simple phrase is, “I’d like a few minutes. Can we continue at seven?”

Agent: Which conversation habit would you both like to try first?

[Wait for agreement. Ask separately who owns the shared commitment and when they will review it. Record their exact choices.]

12:00–14:00 | MAKING PROGRESS VISIBLE

Agent: Here is how we could keep track of what is changing. At the start, and again at agreed check-ins, each of you answers a few short questions on your own. “I feel heard.” “I feel respected.” “Our next step is clear.” “We return calmly after a pause.” Zero means never, five means about half the time, and ten means consistently.

Agent: We keep both answers visible. We can also show a shared average, with equal weight for each of you. Then we add a real example from the week, because the story helps explain the score.

Agent: In the sample tracker, the son’s feeling-heard score begins at four and reaches eight. The mother’s begins at six and reaches eight. The shared average moves from five to eight. That is an example of the view we can create over time.

Agent: For your own first review, we would use the answers you give today. Alongside those scores, we can count completed check-ins, shared commitments, and occasions when you returned to a conversation after a pause.

Agent: Which one of those questions would you most like us to pay attention to first?

[Listen. If this is a live debrief, collect each person’s starting response separately and preserve both values.]

14:00–15:00 | A WARM, SPECIFIC CLOSE

Agent: Let me bring together what you have chosen.

[Read back their actual priority, conversation habit, commitment owner and review day. Use short sentences and invite a correction or addition.]

Agent: You have different strengths to draw on, and you can use those differences to make the next conversation more useful for both of you. A clear question, a little curiosity, and a small agreement can give you something meaningful to practice.

Agent: Before we finish, I’d love each of you to name one thing you appreciate about how the other person approached this conversation. Kieren, would you like to begin?

[Give both people a brief turn.]

Agent: Thank you, both. I’m looking forward to hearing what you notice when you try your agreement this week.
